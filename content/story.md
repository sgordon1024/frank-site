# The Tale of Frank

## Chapter One: A Very Small Dog Takes Up Residence

Frank was born on the tenth of July in the year two thousand twenty-six, orange of coat and modest of size, weighing scarcely more than a well-fed squirrel. The records of the day list him plainly as a Chihuahua mix, Animal ID 26-1568, which is a great deal of paperwork for three pounds of dog.

His mother is a dog named Ducky, who travels with a nomad named Arlo, which means Frank was born to the road before he ever chose it. From Arlo's camp he came into the care of one Steve Gordon and was carried home to a most unusual estate: a van. Not a house with a van, mind you. The van is the house. It travels the high desert of Oregon, and Frank travels with it, a very small dog with a very large territory.

He answers to Frank. Depending on the day and the crime, he also answers to Frankie, Frankenstein, Frank and Beans, Frank the Tank, and Frank Costanza. Mostly he answers to the sound of a treat bag.

## Chapter Two: The Education of Frank

Frank's schooling follows the old wisdom: exercise, then discipline, then affection, then rest, and always in that order. His teacher keeps a calm and steady energy, for Frank reads moods the way sailors read weather.

His crate is his den. No one sends Frank to it. A treat sails in, tossed and never handed, and Frank follows it of his own free will, because a den you choose is a castle and a den you are put in is a jail. When he protests at bedtime, as young gentlemen do, the household waits for the pause between complaints before offering any comfort. Frank is learning that quiet, not commotion, opens negotiations.

In matters of plumbing he has become something of a prodigy. Whole days now pass without incident, thanks to a strict rhythm of den, adventure, and a prompt trip to the designated grounds, where all praise in the household is exclusively spent. At night he holds his post a full four hours, which for a bladder the size of an apricot is heroic work.

## Chapter Three: The Sour Spray Incident

It came to pass that Frank developed a taste for chewing on things that were not his. The household procured a spray of legendary sourness, applied it generously, and waited for the lesson to land.

Frank licked it. Frank liked it. Frank came back for seconds.

The bottle has been retired with full honors, and a sterner potion called Bitter Apple now guards the household's cords and corners, which are themselves wrapped in armor of split loom tubing. The score stands: Frank 1, Sour Spray 0.

## Chapter Four: The Great Zipper Escape

On a quiet evening in September, the household discovered Frank at large. The crate stood closed. The crate also stood empty.

An investigation revealed the method: the zipper. Frank, unsupervised for a moment and ambitious for many more, had worked the door open like a small orange locksmith and let himself out into the world, which for him is a van, which meant the escape covered roughly six feet. He seemed enormously pleased anyway.

A carabiner now stands watch over the zipper. Frank has not yet defeated the carabiner. The household remains vigilant.

## Chapter Five: What Comes Next

Frank grows. Three pounds became four and a half, and four and a half will not stay put either. There are boosters to collect through October, a proper introduction to the wider world once his shots are done, and a season ahead with his friend Amy while Steve is away.

Somewhere down the road there is a bigger dog in him. The paperwork will still say Chihuahua mix, but the van knows the truth: this is Frank's territory, all of it, from the driver's seat to the horizon.
